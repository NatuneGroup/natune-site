Features

Built for people who listen closely

Serious audio plumbing under a friendly surface. Pick your sources once — Natune handles the rest.

Multi-source lossless playback

Stream FLAC and hi-res audio from Tidal, Qobuz and Deezer, with YouTube and Telegram as fallbacks. Sources play in the priority order you choose — disabled sources are skipped everywhere: playback, prefetch, crossfade and downloads.

Tidal · FLAC Qobuz · 24-bit Deezer · FLAC Telegram YouTube · fallback

Audiophile-grade DSP

System equalizer and spatial audio, EBU R128 loudness normalization, tempo and pitch controls — plus bit-perfect output over USB DACs when you want the stream untouched.

Smart transitions & automix

Crossfade and automix between tracks with bounded hand-off and recovery policies — gapless listening that survives bad networks.

Word-by-word lyrics

Synced, TTML and karaoke lyrics powered by Lyprio, with translation and romanization where available.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Natune free?
Yes — completely free and open source under GPL-3.0. No ads, no paywalled features, no tracking. Some streaming sources (like Tidal or Qobuz) require your own paid subscription with that provider.
Is Natune legal? What sources does it use?
Natune is a media player, not a music service — it does not host, upload, or distribute any content. It plays from sources you configure and authenticate with: Tidal, Qobuz, Deezer, YouTube, Telegram and others, using your own accounts and subscriptions where required. How you use it is your responsibility; check the terms of each provider.
Does it really play lossless audio?
Yes — where the source and your subscription provide it, Natune streams FLAC up to hi-res (24-bit/192 kHz on supported services like Tidal and Qobuz). With a USB DAC you can enable bit-perfect output so the stream reaches the DAC unaltered.
Do I need accounts for Tidal, Qobuz or Deezer?
For full-quality streams, yes — each provider requires its own account and, for lossless tiers, a paid subscription. YouTube works without a sign-in for basic playback, and Telegram uses your own Telegram account. You choose which sources are enabled and in which order they are tried.
What is the Playback Pool?
ArchivePool is a self-hostable community pool: contributors anonymously share Tidal/Qobuz instances and accounts, the pool health-checks them on a schedule and drops dead ones, and Natune auto-discovers the surviving sources at runtime. It is optional — the app works fine with only your own accounts.
Is there an iOS or desktop version?
In the works. Natune KMP — a Kotlin Multiplatform port for Windows, macOS, Linux and iOS — is under active development in NatuneGroup/ArchiveTune-KMP. There is no public beta date yet; watch the news feed for announcements.
Stable or Nightly — which should I install?
Stable is the recommended channel: tested releases you can always grab from releases/latest. Nightly builds (tagged N…) ship every merge — newest features, occasional bugs. You can install both side by side if the package names differ, otherwise keep a backup before switching.
What data does Natune send?
Very little by default. The news feed is fetched from this site with a plain anonymous GET — no identifiers, no telemetry. The optional “listener presence” feature is strictly opt-in: while enabled and playing, the app sends a random rotating ID, platform and app version about every 15 minutes; country/city are derived server-side from IP and rounded to ~1 km. It never sends what you play, your accounts, or your device ID — and opting out deletes the local ID. See PRIVACY.md for details.
How do I report a bug or request a feature?
Open an issue on GitHub — include your app version, Android version and steps to reproduce. For chat support, join the Telegram group or Discord server.
Can I donate or support the project?
The most valuable contribution is a good bug report, a translation, or a pull request. If you want to support development financially, the maintainer accepts donations via GitHub Sponsors and Ko-fi.
Does Natune work on Android TV?
Yes — the same APK supports Android TV with a leanback-friendly interface, so you can browse and play from the couch.
Can I back up or move my library to a new phone?
Yes. Natune can export a backup of your settings, library and playlists from Settings → Backup, and restore it on another device or after a reinstall.
