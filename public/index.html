How a song gets to your ears

Natune is a player, not a music service. It looks for the song in the sources you turned on, in the order you set, and plays the best copy it finds.

  1. 1

    You choose the sources

    Tidal, Qobuz, Deezer, YouTube, Telegram and local files. Put them in your order. A source you turn off is skipped everywhere: playback, prefetch, crossfade and downloads.

  2. 2

    Natune finds the best stream

    Each track is matched across your sources and the highest quality you can play wins, up to 24-bit/192 kHz FLAC. If one source fails, the next one takes over without stopping the music.

  3. 3

    Sound is shaped your way

    Equalizer, loudness normalization, tempo and pitch, crossfade and beat-matched automix. With a USB DAC, bit-perfect output leaves the stream untouched.

Questions

Is Natune free?
Yes. It is free and open source under GPL-3.0, with no ads and no paid features. Some sources, like Tidal or Qobuz, need your own subscription with that service.
Where does the music come from?
From the sources you set up: Tidal, Qobuz, Deezer, YouTube, Telegram, local files and others, using your own accounts where a service requires one. Natune does not host, upload or distribute any media. Check each service's terms for how you may use it.
Does it really play lossless?
Yes, when the source and your subscription provide it: FLAC up to 24-bit/192 kHz. With a USB DAC you can turn on bit-perfect output so the stream reaches the DAC unchanged.
What is the Playback Pool?
An optional, self-hostable community pool of Tidal and Qobuz instances. The pool checks them on a schedule and Natune discovers the working ones at runtime. Natune works fine without it.
Stable or Nightly?
Stable releases are tested builds and the right choice for most people. Nightlies are built from the development branch every day: newest features, occasional bugs. Both are on GitHub releases. Make a backup before switching.
What data does Natune send?
Very little. The news feed is a plain anonymous request to this site. The listener map is off until you turn it on; then, only while music plays, the app sends a random ID, the platform and the app version about every 15 minutes, and the server derives a rough location from the IP without storing it. It never sends what you play or your accounts. See PRIVACY.md.
Can I move my library to a new phone?
Yes. Settings → Backup exports your settings, library and playlists, and you can restore the file on another device.
How do I report a bug?
Open an issue on GitHub with your app version, platform and the steps that cause it. For chat, join Telegram or Discord.
